# Predictions for bench pass 5 and for the production operating points

Written 2026-10-23, before pass 5 ran, from the simulator at the state preserved in this repository (calibration as on the
Calibration page). The point of this file is to be scored afterwards: every line is a number the pass
will measure. Model numbers carry the ±25 % the validation set showed; where Strata's own published
measurements disagree with the model, both are given and the bet is marked.

Box: i9-10900X (AVX-512, 4-ch DDR4-3200), 3 x RTX 3090 at 250 W (two at x16, one at x8),
RTX 3060 x4; Strata 0.1.43.1, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next IQ3_S, int8 KV, 32K context, parking 8 GiB / 8,
thinking sent back by the bench client. Decode at short context (≤ 4K) unless stated.

Falsifiable statements, in order of confidence:

1. Prompt reading on three cards is 15–25 % faster than on two (4,000–4,600 vs 3,300). *High.*
2. A single conversation writes no faster on three cards than on two at ≤ 4K context (±5 %), because the
   expert cache is already complete on two cards. *High.* (At 64K context the third card helps, as pass 1 saw.)
3. With two slots, one serial group with `--batch-mtp` beats the pipelined default. *Medium-high*; Strata's own
   2 x 3080 numbers show +15 %.
4. With four slots on two cards, the pipelined default beats one serial group with `--batch-mtp`. *Medium*: the
   model says the opposite (+45 %), Strata's header says −25–30 % on 2 x 3090; 0.2.1's A4000 data went the other
   way. This is the single most informative measurement of the pass for the model.
5. Six slots on three cards reach 220–300 tok/s total. *Medium.* Below 200 would mean the CPU pool or
   hand-offs cost more than modelled; above 300 would mean rows share experts better than assumed.
6. `--pipeline-windows 2` at one slot: decode within ±5 %, prompt +10–20 %. *Medium*, from Strata's 2 x 3090 report;
   the model does not simulate it.
7. The expert-cache hit stays ≥ 0.98 in every cell; if it drops below 0.95 with six slots, the slot cost per card is
   larger than the 0.56 GiB assumed.

## 2. Production operating points (Turnstone as configured today)

Strata on the two x16 cards with `--batch 4`, 27B on the x8 card (vLLM AWQ, 4 seqs), guard 1 x 8K and judge
1 x 32K on the 3060, reranker on the CPU, coordinator hosted. Workload = the assumed ranges on the roles page
(20 scenarios). To be scored against `intent_verdicts`, the usage report and the Caddy route logs after a week
of real use; the task throughput depends on the real workload and will only match in order of magnitude.

| workstreams | tasks/h (p10–p90) | task p90 | Strata busy | 27B busy, queue p90 | judge/min, busy | guard/min, refused |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.7–5.2 (2.5) | 28 min | 11 % | 41 %, 20 s | 1.8, 27 % | 2.1, 21 of ~250 (8 %) |
| 2 | 0.6–4.4 (2.6) | 58 min | 11 % | 63 %, 78 s | 2.2, 39 % | 2.4, 38 |
| 4 | 0.6–5.0 (2.6) | 111 min | 13 % | 86 %, 195 s | 2.4, 49 % | 2.6, 82 |

With parking (8 GiB / 8 conversations) the maker's re-read share falls from 45–55 % to ~21 % at 2–4
workstreams; throughput does not move, because the 27B card is the bottleneck. Coordinator cost: $0.03–0.11
per three-task run at Lyceum's prices.

What to check first in production: (a) the 27B card is the first resource to saturate as workstreams grow;
(b) judge calls land at 2–3 per minute with a 25 % approval share, and the ratio judge-per-minute / guard-per-minute
is ~0.9; (c) about 8 % of guard calls exceed an 8K window.

## 3. Scoring

For each cell: measured vs model and vs bet; a cell counts as a hit within ±25 % of the model or inside the bet
range. Then: which of statements 1–7 held. Cells that miss by more than 40 % become new CAL or VAL points in
`backend/app/calibration.py`, and the constants that move (pipelined-group overhead, batch-mtp acceptance,
third-card prompt scaling, slot VRAM cost) are refitted with the two-card cells held out.
